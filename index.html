I'm Uriah, an embedded firmware engineer focused on microcontrollers, low-power design, and robotics. My work connects firmware with the circuits, sensors, actuators, and people around it.

I started at sixteen in Chinle, Arizona, repairing phones and laptops. Five years of finding hardware faults taught me to isolate one subsystem at a time and check the behavior on the bench.

At Microchip Technology, I moved from applications support and automation to PIC18 examples and PIC32CM PL10 low-power labs. At ASU, I integrated an edge-AI soil monitor, led a modular eyewear team, and analyzed a critical-facility microgrid. I test assumptions against the hardware and constraints at hand.

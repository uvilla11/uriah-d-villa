OPERATIONAL PHILOSOPHY

Engineering principles.

01

Understand the constraint first.

Architecture must follow physical reality: power budgets, thermal dissipation, pin multiplexing, bus bandwidth, and schedule limits outrank ideal models.

02

Prefer observable systems.

If a state, bus transaction, or timing jitter cannot be measured with a probe or logic analyzer, the system is not yet under control.

03

Build the smallest reliable abstraction.

Complexity must justify itself. A clean, direct hardware register configuration is always preferable to opaque framework layers.

04

Document tradeoffs honestly.

Every technical decision has a cost. A design is stronger when its failure modes, limitations, and operational boundaries are explicitly acknowledged.

Designing for Unknown Surface Topography

Because asteroid 16 Psyche has never been visited by a lander, surface properties—granularity, friction, and slope—remain uncharacterized. The mechanical architecture paired a self-righting rounded chassis with six outward-opening panels that stabilize the platform regardless of orientation.

The embedded challenge was to orient an internal micro-rover toward the single exit with the clearest departure trajectory, lock the indexed platform in place, and actuate the corresponding door with zero ambiguity.

Dual-Controller Architecture on a Custom KiCad PCB

I designed a unified 2-layer PCB in KiCad integrating the entire power supply, motor drivers, sensor signal conditioning, and dual microcontrollers:

  • PIC18F MCU: Handled the state machine, rotational closed-loop motor control, and interrupt service routines triggered by Hall sensors.
  • PSoC 4 MCU: Managed independent servo deployment for the six chassis panels, external illumination, and fault telemetry.
  • Magnetic Alignment: Six neodymium magnets mounted at 60° increments around the turntable, providing contactless, dust-resistant position markers.
The defining engineering triumph was not a perfect first board—it was diagnosing an under-rated driver and noisy Hall sensor lines on the bench, and executing a revised PCB v1.1 that withstood 500+ uninterrupted cycles.

Bench Debugging & Revision Iteration

Initial testing revealed inductive noise spikes during motor startup that triggered false Hall interrupts. I added RC filtering and op-amp hysteresis to condition the signal cleanly before the MCU inputs. Additionally, the motor driver was upgraded to a higher peak-current package with thermal copper pours on PCB v1.1.

Validation Summary & Boundary Conditions

The physical turntable achieved ±2.0° positioning error over 500+ test runs. The lander was tested on an adjustable incline up to 36°, far exceeding the 20° project requirement.

*Note: SolidWorks FEA impact simulations indicated a 2.794 safety factor under a 2 m/s impact with titanium-alloy assumptions; the physical prototype tested on Earth was manufactured from steel and aluminum for cost efficiency.

The Hardware-to-Model Feature Mismatch

The project goal was a low-cost, off-grid diagnostic tool for agricultural extension workers in resource-constrained environments. Initial research datasets relied on chemical nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium (NPK) measurements—sensors that were physically absent on the selected T-Higrow evaluation board.

Rather than relying on synthetic data or unavailable sensors, I led the decision to re-train the Edge Impulse classification model exclusively on features the physical board could measure: capacitive soil moisture, electrical conductivity (EC), and temperature.

Firmware Architecture & Local Dashboard

I integrated the generated C++ inference library into the ESP32 Arduino framework, bridging sensor sampling, feature normalization, neural inference, and real-time dashboard updates:

  • Configured the ESP32 in standalone SoftAP mode, serving an interactive ESP-DASH web interface over local Wi-Fi without needing an internet gateway.
  • Optimized memory buffers to prevent FreeRTOS stack overflow between the web server and tensor arena.
  • Surfaced instantaneous classification probabilities and actionable remediation advice on the local display.

Showcase Demonstration & Honest Boundaries

The completed system was demonstrated at the 2025 ASU Innovation Showcase using controlled soil calibration samples. While the project demonstrated proof-of-concept feasibility, it represents a controlled course prototype rather than a ruggedized, field-validated agricultural instrument.

The Clinical Challenge: Variable Visual Stress

Patients recovering from stroke, concussion, or traumatic brain injury frequently experience debilitating photophobia and spatial disorientation. Commercial sunglasses offer only static, broadband attenuation that fails to adapt to shifting indoor/outdoor lighting or therapeutic exercise needs.

ShadeShift unified four distinct clinical modalities into a single wearable frame: gradable electrochromic tint, interchangeable color wavelength filters, liquid-crystal stroboscopic training, and magnetic binasal occlusion plates.

Pivoting from PNLC to PDLC & Mass Optimization

Bench testing exposed two major engineering hurdles:

  • Material Response: Polymer Network Liquid Crystal (PNLC) films suffered from slow optical decay times, causing blurry stroboscopic pulses. We transitioned to Polymer Dispersed Liquid Crystal (PDLC) driven by high-voltage AC square waves, achieving crisp transitions up to 15 Hz.
  • Ergonomic Weight: Mounting the LiPo battery and boost circuitry onto the glasses frame created excessive facial pressure. We redesigned the architecture to externalize all power and electronics into a pocket-sized tethered controller.

Research Boundary: Engineering vs. Clinical Efficacy

ShadeShift successfully demonstrated mechanical and electro-optical integration. As documented in our peer-reviewed research paper, clinical efficacy, patient trials, and medical device safety certifications remain designated for future clinical phases.

When Mathematical Optimization Collides with Physical Space

Queen’s Medical Center represents the premier Level I trauma facility in the Pacific Basin, operating under high Hawaiian electric utility tariffs and extreme island vulnerability to natural disasters.

When ran without explicit physical area constraints, the optimizer yielded a massive 4.98 MW solar PV array and a 12.3 MWh battery storage system. While this minimized calculated Net Present Cost, a spatial audit confirmed that an urban medical campus simply does not have the contiguous land or structural roof capacity for a 5 MW solar installation.

Model Validation: Rebuilding with Real Constraints

Rather than presenting the unconstrained optimum as a completed engineering recommendation, I remodeled the microgrid with explicit physical boundaries:

  • Capped rooftop solar PV to 1.2 MW based on structural engineering estimates.
  • Integrated existing diesel generation assets for multi-day catastrophic outage survival.
  • Right-sized the battery energy storage system (BESS) for peak-shaving and critical life-support transition loads.

Conclusion: Engineering Beyond the Solver

This study demonstrated that an optimization model is only as valid as its representation of physical boundary conditions. The resulting hybrid microgrid architecture provided realistic, defensible resilience for life-safety systems.

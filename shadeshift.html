ShadeShift

This case covers the PNLC prototype phase of modular eyewear exploring tint, hue, strobe, and binasal occlusion. Material response and wearability remained constraints, not validated clinical outcomes.

  • ASU team project · 2025
  • ESP32 · PNLC prototype · 3D printing
  • Team prototype demo
ProblemRole and teamDesign decisionsImplementationValidationLimitsSources

Problem

Bring several visual modes into a wearable proof of concept without putting all the electronics on the frame. The liquid-crystal film's response varied with temperature and voltage, limiting reliable stroboscopic switching.

Role and team outcome

My role

Led the five-author project team and worked on electronics integration, enclosure direction, and modular prototyping. The demo was published under my account.

Team outcome

The team assembled and demonstrated printed eyewear with interchangeable attachments. The paper documents the prototype and its unresolved response and testing limits; this was not a clinical study.

Constraint and decision

Use a tethered external-electronics design because the temple arms lacked space for all the components. This limits frame load but adds cable-management constraints. Use interchangeable printed attachments for the optical modes.

Implementation

The project explored liquid-crystal switching, ESP32 tint prototyping, an external strobe controller, and printed modular frame parts. The paper includes external-enclosure CAD. That design is distinct from a demonstrated assembled control box, which is not visible in the photograph.

Validation

The team demonstrated the four-mode concept and documented film latency as a challenge. No project-specific, instrument-verified switching-time or clinical-efficacy figure from the paper is presented as a passed result.

Limits

The paper uses both PNLC and PDLC terminology without resolving the phase chronology. This case therefore stays within the PNLC account and unresolved latency. It does not claim a validated later material change or a 10 Hz result. The supplied sources do not establish clinical efficacy, electrical safety, long-term wearability, or clinical trials.

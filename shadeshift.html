ShadeShift

A modular eyewear prototype explored tint, hue, strobe, and binasal occlusion. Material response and wearability remained design constraints, not solved clinical outcomes.

  • ASU team project · 2025
  • ESP32 · PNLC prototype · 3D printing
  • Team prototype demo
ProblemRole and teamDesign decisionsImplementationValidationLimitsSources

Problem

Bring several visual modes into a wearable proof of concept without putting all the electronics on the frame. The liquid-crystal film's response varied with temperature and voltage, limiting reliable stroboscopic switching.

Role and team outcome

My role

Led the five-author project team and worked on electronics integration, enclosure direction, and modular prototyping. The demo was published under my account.

Team outcome

The team assembled and demonstrated printed eyewear with interchangeable attachments. The paper documents the prototype and its unresolved response and testing limits; this was not a clinical study.

Constraint and decision

Move the electronics to an external enclosure to limit frame weight. Use interchangeable 3D-printed attachments for the optical modes. Keep switching-speed optimization an open engineering problem rather than reporting a later PDLC result not established by the documented prototype.

Implementation

Prototyped with PNLC film, an ESP32 breadboard setup, printed frame components, and a separate control enclosure. The available paper and photograph show build stages and assembled hardware, not verified clinical effects.

Validation

The team demonstrated the four-mode concept and documented film latency as a challenge. No project-specific, instrument-verified switching-time or clinical-efficacy figure from the paper is presented as a passed result.

Limits

The paper's prototype used PNLC film and lists response improvements as future work. This page does not assert a completed PDLC switch or a 10 Hz validation result. Clinical efficacy, electrical safety, long-term wearability, and trials were not established.

SolarSENSE

An offline soil-sensing prototype made a model, actual board inputs, and a local dashboard work together. The firmware integration mattered more than a single accuracy headline.

  • ASU team project · 2025
  • LILYGO T-Higrow ESP32 · Edge Impulse
  • Team repository
ProblemRole and teamDesign decisionsImplementationValidationLimitsSources

Problem

Use on-device inference to present soil-condition information without relying on an internet connection. The early NPK-oriented dataset and the physical sensors available on the chosen board did not describe the same inputs.

Role and team outcome

My role

Integrated the teammate-trained Edge Impulse model into ESP32 firmware and the device-hosted ESP DASH interface. My contribution was firmware/dashboard integration, not sole ownership of model training.

Team outcome

The team presented a soil-sensing proof of concept at ASU. The linked repository belongs to teammate Jacob Pisors and does not independently attribute commits to me.

Constraint and decision

The LILYGO T-Higrow provides moisture and salinity-related readings, not a direct NPK measurement. Match the inference path to inputs the device can actually sample instead of implying it measures nutrients it cannot sense.

Implementation

Joined the Edge Impulse inference export with the sensor firmware and a local ESP DASH view hosted on the ESP32. The public team repository contains firmware and model-export packages; the integration is described here as team work with my role stated separately.

Validation

The team demonstrated the prototype with potted-soil samples and a local dashboard. The available reports discuss multiple datasets and model iterations; one headline accuracy percentage would hide that distinction.

Limits

This was a controlled course prototype, not a deployment to farms or a direct NPK analyzer. Sensor position and soil conditions affect the interpretation; no single model-accuracy claim is promoted here.

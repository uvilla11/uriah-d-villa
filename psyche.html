Hephaestus landing system

A lander concept needed a repeatable way to position six deployment destinations. I led the embedded electronics and firmware work inside a larger ASU team project associated with NASA Psyche.

Problem

A six-door deployment platform requires a repeatable reference for rotary alignment. The sensor and motor-driver circuitry must keep delivering usable signals after mechanical and PCB revisions.

Role and team outcome

My role

Led the electronics and firmware subsystem: custom PCB, Hall-effect position sensing, and motor-control integration.

Team outcome

The ASU team designed and built the broader lander prototype. The official Psyche capstone listing names Uriah Villa among the robotics students; it does not independently certify subsystem test figures.

Constraint and decision

Use a physical Hall-effect reference at each destination instead of assuming the motor command alone establishes position. Revise the board and signal path as the prototype exposes motor-driver and sensing problems.

Implementation

Integrated a PIC18/PSoC-controlled positioning system with a custom PCB and six sensed destinations. The team revised the PCB after testing identified an under-rated motor driver and Hall-signal conditioning needs.

Validation

±2°project-reported rotation accuracy across six destinationsAlso reported in my project materials: 500+ automated positioning cycles. These are project-reported test figures, not numbers supplied by the public Psyche listing.

The team's prototype was also evaluated on an incline and through structural simulation. Those team-level mechanical results are distinct from my electronics and firmware contribution.

Limits

This was an academic proof of concept, not a NASA flight-qualified system. The 500+ cycles and ±2° figures come from project reporting. The official Psyche listing establishes team membership and scope, not those quantitative results.

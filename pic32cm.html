PIC32CM low-power labs

From a standby baseline to event-driven light sensing. The public four-lab example and follow-up technical brief use different measurement setups; each tells a separate part of the power story.

Problem

Show what standby alone saves, then remove periodic CPU work from a sensor application without hiding the measurement conditions. The labs progress from a bare standby baseline through an active implementation and standby with interrupts to autonomous peripheral operation.

Role and team outcome

My role

Built out the four firmware labs, documentation, and Lab 4 power-configuration revision. Public commits record my initial Lab 1 project on January 22, 2026, Labs 2-4 on February 10, and the power revision on March 25. I also authored the follow-up TB3388 brief.

Team outcome

Microchip published the lab repository and companion technical brief. A colleague created the initial repository and CI scaffold. This is not presented as a solo company release.

Constraint and decision

The sensor still has to sample ambient light and report a threshold event. Periodically waking the CPU to orchestrate sampling defeats much of the intended standby saving. Move the timing, trigger, conversion, and transfer chain to peripherals. Minimize CPU work and qualify UART reporting against the light threshold. The intended wake policy is distinct from proving the wake cadence of a specific checked-in revision.

Implementation

The published sequence uses RTC, the event system, timers, ADC, DMA, and UART. Lab 2 is the active-mode reference; Lab 3 adds standby with interrupts; Lab 4 lets the peripheral chain do repeated sampling while the CPU sleeps. The repository's March revision explicitly updates Lab 4 configuration to improve power consumption. After the first lab iteration, I pursued lower current in the working light-sensing application for the follow-up brief. It still prints threshold events to a terminal. That Atmel Power Debugger setup is reported separately from the repository monitor.

Limits and comparability

The repository's ~559 µA is a blended average; the brief separates below-threshold, above-threshold, and between-wake windows. The active-mode clocks and measurement hardware also differ. The public sources do not isolate which setup or firmware difference accounts for the gap, so these readings are not a controlled before-and-after instrument comparison. Within the brief, one Atmel Power Debugger setup measures the firmware architecture's Active-to-SleepWalking reduction.

The README and brief mix an ADC result-ready/TC2 wake description with threshold-only language. A chosen firmware revision needs a wake-cadence check; the published average alone does not establish that every below-threshold sample avoids a CPU wake.

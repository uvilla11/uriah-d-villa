PIC32CM low-power labs

From a standby baseline to event-driven light sensing. The public four-lab example and follow-up technical brief use different measurement setups; each tells a separate part of the power story.

Problem

Show what standby alone saves, then remove periodic CPU work from a sensor application without hiding the measurement conditions. The labs progress from a bare standby baseline through an active implementation and standby with interrupts to autonomous peripheral operation.

Role and team outcome

My role

Built out the four firmware labs, documentation, and Lab 4 power-configuration revision. The public commit history attributes the lab additions and later revision to Uriah Villa.

Team outcome

Microchip published the lab repository and companion technical brief. A colleague created the initial repository and CI scaffold; this is not presented as a solo company release.

Constraint and decision

The sensor still has to sample ambient light and report a threshold event. Periodically waking the CPU to orchestrate sampling defeats much of the intended standby saving. Move the timing, trigger, conversion, and transfer chain to peripherals; reserve a CPU wake for the window-comparator event and UART output.

Implementation

The published sequence uses RTC, the event system, timers, ADC, DMA, and UART. Lab 2 is the active-mode reference; Lab 3 adds standby with interrupts; Lab 4 lets the peripheral chain do repeated sampling while the CPU sleeps. The repository's March revision explicitly updates Lab 4 configuration to improve power consumption. TB3388 later explains the same architectural progression as a companion brief.

Limits and comparability

The repository's ~559 µA is a blended average; the brief separates below-threshold, above-threshold, and between-wake windows. The active-mode clocks and measurement hardware also differ. The public sources do not isolate which setup or firmware difference accounts for the gap, so these readings are not a controlled before-and-after instrument comparison. Within the brief, one Atmel Power Debugger setup measures the firmware architecture's Active-to-SleepWalking reduction.
